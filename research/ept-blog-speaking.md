## 2. Should You Use Idioms in IELTS Speaking? (2026-04)
URL: https://ielts.englishprotips.com/should-you-use-idioms-in-ielts-speaking/
📄 https://ielts.englishprotips.com/wp-content/uploads/2026/04/should-you-use-idioms-ielts-speaking-study-guide.pdf
Thesis: band descriptors reward "idiomatic language" used NATURALLY; memorised/shoe-horned idioms signal poor control and LOWER scores. Avoid obscure list-idioms ("once in a blue moon", "spill the beans", "raining cats and dogs" in a climate-change answer...). Instead: (1) common phrases (to be honest/fair, what I mean is, it depends really); (2) collocations (make a decision, take your time, strong opinion, deeply concerned); (3) simple expressions used well (that said, having grown up with…, I suppose what strikes me most is…). DO: everyday phrases, collocations. DON'T: memorised lists, unsure usage.
Quiz (10Q correct/incorrect usage): Correct / Incorrect (hit the books = study, not hobby) / Correct / Incorrect (let hair down ≠ quiet library) / Correct (clear my mind) / Incorrect (paint the town red ≠ early bed) / Correct / Correct / Correct / Correct.

## 10. Make Fewer Grammar Mistakes — a/an/the (2024-01)
URL: https://ielts.englishprotips.com/make-fewer-grammar-mistakes-a-an-the/
📄 https://drive.google.com/file/d/1_XGof0RrYIybKKvN1MxOKCoxGAdAMNJf/view?usp=sharing
Rules: (1) Ø for generals ("Ø exams pressure Ø students"); (2) the = specific ("The exams... in the final year..."); (3) the + adjective = group ("The rich..."); (4) a + consonant sound; (5) an + vowel sound.
Cloze ✅: Ø/Ø / The+the / The / A+Ø / Ø+an+Ø.
