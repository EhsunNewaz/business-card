## 1) Lesson 1 — Course Outline and Content (full text)

Congratulations on taking this step towards improving your IELTS vocabulary. This course is
designed to teach you advanced vocabulary which has helped IELTS candidates get a band 7+.
We have organised the vocabulary into different themes related to the IELTS exam.

**How long will this course take?** This course has 20 lessons. The number of lessons you
choose to do per day will depend on your level of English and how long you have to prepare
for your IELTS exam. In general, we recommend that you do between 1-2 lessons per day.
As a result, this course usually takes between 10 and 20 days. We also recommend that you
regularly come back and revise the lessons — this is essential if you want to learn all
the vocabulary from this course.

## 2) Lesson 2 — How to Increase your Vocabulary (full text)

- Duration: 15 minutes. Video alt source:
  https://iframe.mediadelivery.net/play/220384/caae8277-0bdc-43ce-bd9d-39bee330047e
- PDF (login-walled): "5-Step Method For Learning Vocabulary"
  (https://englishprotips.com/mp-files/5-step-method-for-learning-vocabulary.pdf/)

**Intro:** In this lesson, we look at what you can do to improve your vocabulary.
"How to learn vocabulary quickly and effectively"

**1. Watch a lot of English speaking videos and films.** One of the best ways to improve
your vocabulary is to watch lots of English speaking films, videos, and series. You should
watch these **with English subtitles**. If you see a word or phrase which you find
interesting, write it down.
- **YouTube:** Accent videos (different English accents); English learning channels: EngVid,
  English with Lucy, MmmEnglish; IELTS channels: English Speaking Academy, E2 IELTS,
  IELTS Liz, IELTS Advantage; Interesting videos: whatever you're interested in.
- **Series:** Friends, Gossip Girl, How I Met Your Mother, Downton Abbey.
- **Movies** (not too complicated, not too much talking): The Dark Knight, Notting Hill,
  The Pursuit of Happiness, Men in Black.

**2. Read books and articles in English.** Read for fun! …constantly checking words in
dictionaries and writing words in vocabulary books will make reading boring and you will
soon give up. Make it enjoyable.
- **Books:** Harry Potter; Sapiens; Shoe Dog (Memoir by the Creator of Nike);
  Roald Dahl's short stories.
- **Magazines:** BBC, The Economist, The Guardian, The Wall Street Journal.
  (*Many of the examiners get inspiration from these magazines, so by reading these
  regularly you are more likely to learn vocabulary and ideas related to common
  IELTS topics.)

**3. Use and review the vocabulary you learn.** To learn a new word use/review it 7 times
over a long period of time: learn (day 1) → review night of day 1 → review night of
day 2 → skip a day, review day 4 → day 8 → day 16 → day 32. Set phone/calendar reminders.
Spacing out review increases retention.

**4. Language learning apps** (use anywhere: train, queue…): Bussuu (courses + native
feedback), Duolingo (fun quizzes/courses), Quizlet (games/quizzes), HelloTalk (exchange),
Babbel (courses/quizzes), IELTS Prep (tests/activities).

**5. Learn vocabulary related to IELTS.** "It's important to know the vocabulary that is
relevant to the IELTS exam. That's what we have aimed to do in this course. We have
selected the words and phrases which are the most useful for talking about a range of
common topics in IELTS. These are words which have helped students get a band 7, 8, and 9
in their speaking and writing test. Each lesson has quizzes designed to show you how each
of the words can be used in a typical IELTS sentence. Best of luck!"

Also: learning English from songs → lyricstraining.com.

## 3) Lesson 3 — Describing People (full content)

- Duration: 1 hour. Tabs: Video | Lesson Material | Practice.
- Video alt source:
  https://iframe.mediadelivery.net/play/220384/a67a024e-f697-4161-9362-7beb9af5eea4

  (https://englishprotips.com/mp-files/new-advanced-vocabulary-for-describing-people.pdf/)

### 3a) Word list (20 words) — full entries

1. **The kind/type of (person) who… (informal phrase)** /ðə.kaɪndə.pəsən.hu/ —
   A phrase for describing typical behaviour of someone.
   _He's the kind of person who goes to bed at the same time every night._
2. **Composed (adjective)** /kəmˈpəʊzd/ — Calm and in control of your emotions.
   _It's important to stay composed if you get asked a tricky question during the IELTS
   speaking exam._ Collocation: calm and composed. Compose (verb): _take a deep breath
   and try to compose yourself._ Composure (noun, U): _don't lose your composure._
3. **Materialistic (adjective)** /məˌtɪərɪəˈlɪstɪk/ — Believing money and possessions are
   most important. _Older generations feel that the youth are too materialistic: always
   shopping…_ Materialistic + society/person/generation. Materialism (n): _greed and
   materialism of modern society._
4. **Rapport (noun, U)** /rep'or/ — Good understanding + ability to communicate well.
   _It's important for teachers to develop a good rapport with their students._
   Verb + rapport: build/establish; Adj + rapport: friendly/good/close.
5. **Diligent (adjective)** /ˈdɪlɪdʒ(ə)nt/ — Careful, using a lot of effort.
   _Diligent students tend to do better in exams than those that try and cram last minute._
   diligent + student. Diligence (n, U); diligently (adv).
6. **Status symbol (noun, C)** /ˈsteɪ.təsɪmbəl/ — Something wanted because others will
   respect you for it. _Cars act as a status symbol for many people._ act as / represent
   + status symbol.
7. **Inquisitive (adjective)** /ɪnˈkwɪzɪtɪv/ — Wanting to discover as much as possible
   (sometimes annoyingly). _Children should be encouraged to be inquisitive…_
   inquisitive + about; inquisitive + child/student/mind.
8. **Temperament (noun, U)** /ˈtɛmp(ə)rəm(ə)nt/ — The part of character affecting moods
   and behaviour. _My best friend has always had a calm temperament…_
   Adj + temperament: good/calm/fiery/angry/moody. Temperamental (adj):
   _one moment good mood, second later angry._
9. **Have (something) in common with (someone) (idiom)** — Share interests/characteristics.
   _I feel that I have nothing in common with any of my work colleagues._
10. **Studious (adjective)** /ˈstjuːdɪəs/ — Likes/spends long time studying.
    _Teachers tend to like children that are intelligent and studious._
    Studious + child/student/boy/girl. Studiously (adv).
11. **People person (adjective)** — Friendly, enjoys meeting/talking to people.
    _I've never been much of a people person; I prefer to stay at home with a good book._
12. **Demanding (adjective)** /dɪˈmɑːndɪŋ/ — Makes others work hard; not easily satisfied.
    _I have a particularly demanding job…_ demanding + job/boss/course/child/parents.
13. **Role model (noun, C)** /ˈrəʊlmɒd(ə)l/ — Person admired, behaviour copied.
    _Sports stars are role models for thousands of young people._
    Adj + role model: good/bad/excellent.
14. **Get on (with) (phrasal verb)** — Like each other, be friendly.
    _Life can be very difficult if you don't get on with the people you live with._
    (= get along).
15. **Ambitious (adjective)** /amˈbɪʃəs/ — Strong wish to be successful/powerful/rich.
    _Bosses are more likely to notice and promote ambitious staff._
    ambitious + people/teacher/colleague/friend. Ambition (n, U); ambitiously (adv).
16. **Narrow-minded (adjective)** (opp: broad-minded/open-minded) — Not willing to accept
    different ideas. _Young, idealistic people often find older generations to be
    narrow-minded._ Similar: closed-minded.
17. **Idealistic (adjective)** /ʌɪdɪəˈlɪstɪk/ — Believing very good things can be achieved.
    _Young graduates tend to leave university feeling optimistic and idealistic…_
    Idealism (n, U).
18. **Stereotype (noun/adj/verb)** /ˈsteriəʊtaɪp/ — Set idea about what sb/sth is like.
    _Adverts on television are full of cultural stereotypes._
    Verb + stereotype: conform/fit/fill/reinforce/negative. Stereotype (v):
    _sexually stereotyped toys._ Stereotypical (adj): _a stereotypical farmer…_
19. **Charismatic (adjective)** /karɪzˈmatɪk/ — Natural ability to influence/attract.
    _Charismatic teachers are more popular with their students._
    Charismatic + leader/figure/authority. Charisma (n, U).
20. **Optimism / pessimism (noun, U)** — Belief good / bad things will happen.
    _There is a mood of deepening pessimism about the economy._
    Verb + optimism: express/cause. Optimistic/pessimistic (adj).

**Extra — Self+:** self-confident (calm, no self-doubt); self-centred (only interested in
self); self-conscious (worried what people think); self-sufficient/reliant (needs no help);
self-aware (understands self); self-control (control over actions/emotions); self-made
(rich/successful by own hard work).

## 4) Lesson 10 — The Environment (full content)

- Duration: 20 minutes. Tabs: Video | Vocabulary | Practice. Video title:
  "Vocabulary — The environment (compressed)". Alt source:
  https://iframe.mediadelivery.net/play/220384/a3375774-cd88-440d-9326-83a25e1600a0
- PDF (login-walled): https://englishprotips.com/mp-files/the-environment.pdf/

### 4a) Word list (25 words) — full entries

1. **Single-use (adj)** — Used once then thrown away.
   _Individuals should limit their use of single-use packaging._ + bottles/bags/plastics.
2. **Landfill (n, C+U)** — Place where garbage is buried.
   _Landfill is not an acceptable method of waste disposal._ + site.
3. **Reuse (v)** — Use again. _customers often reuse shopping bags._ Reusable (adj).
4. **Sustainable (adj)** — Able to be maintained. _Promoting economic growth is often at
   odds with maintaining a sustainable environment._ Sustain (v); sustainability (n, U).
5. **Conserve (v)** — Keep/protect from waste/loss/damage. _insulate houses to conserve
   heat._ + water/heat/fuel/energy. Conservation (n).
6. **Recycle (v)** — Convert used material for reuse. _schools to recycle all paper._
   Recyclable (adj).
7. **Waste (n, C+U; v)** — Unwanted material after use. _alternative forms of waste
   disposal._ harmful/hazardous/food + waste.
8. **Renewable (adj)** — Easily replaced (solar/wind/hydro). _investing in renewable
   energy…_ Renew (v).
9. **Carbon (dioxide) emissions (n, pl)** — CO₂ from planes/cars/factories.
   _We all need to do more to reduce carbon emissions._
10. **Carbon footprint (n, C)** — CO₂ measure of one's activities. _reduce their carbon
    footprint: reduce electricity/gas, fewer flights._ reduce/increase/create/leave + …
11. **Fossil fuels (n, C)** — Gas/coal/oil. _reduce reliance on fossil fuels._
12. **Global warming (n, U)** — Gradual earth temp rise. _…gone beyond the point of
    no return._
13. **Greenhouse effect (n, C)** — Heat prevented from escaping.
    _increased flooding is a result of the greenhouse effect._ + gas/emissions.
14. **Ozone layer (n, U)** — Oxygen layer blocking UV. _depletion of the ozone layer._
15. **Methane (n, U)** — Greenhouse gas/fuel. _…such as carbon dioxide and methane._
16. **Deforestation (n, U)** — Destroying forests. _destroying large tropical rainforest._
    Deforest (v).
17. **Extinct (adj)** — No longer existing. _giant panda will soon become extinct._
    in danger of / on the verge of + extinct. Extinction (n, U).
18. **Ecosystem (n, C)** — Plants/animals/people + environment as a system.
    _preserve the delicately balanced ecosystem…_ balanced/marine + ecosystem.
19. **Biodiversity (n, U)** — Number/types of life in an area. _protect the biodiversity
    in the Amazon._ Biodiverse (adj).
20. **Degradation (n, U)** — Beauty/quality destroyed. _environmental degradation caused
    by human activity._ Degrade (v).
21. **The food chain (n, U)** — Series of who-eats-whom. _Humans… top of the food chain._
22. **Biodegradable (adj)** — Decays naturally. _biodegradable bags._ Biodegrade (v).
23. **Decompose (v)** — Break into smaller parts. _Waste in landfill… decompose._
    Decomposition (n).
24. **Hazardous (adj)** — Dangerous. _hazardous work/waste/material._ Hazard (n, C).
25. **Contamination (n, U)** — Making dirty/poisonous. _contamination of surroundings._
    avoid/prevent/risk of/source of + contamination. Contaminate (v).
