1. **Simon** (2010-06-10): Hi Adverb, You can definitely talk about a sitcom, but a movie is not really a TV programme. A series is any type of programme with several parts (e.g. a documentary series about history), whereas a serial would be a story that is told in several parts (i.e. drama/fiction but not a history documentary). 4. **Simon** (2010-06-09): Hi Abdul It's true that IELTS is a language test. However, an interesting answer probably contains good ideas and vocabulary. Also, examiners are human! - if the examiner enjoys your presentation, he/she is likely to give you a higher score. In other words, the best students are usually interesting AND use good language. I think your phrase "I'll start the ball rolling by touching on the matter of what the film is and I would probably select..." seems too forced - it seems like you've memorised it, and it's not how a native speaker would begin in this situation ("let's get the ball rolling" is the kind of phrase that people might use to start an informal meeting - it means "I'll start with my idea first"). The phrase "Continuing on the next point, next question regarding when and where I saw this movie..." also seems memorised and 'unnatural'. My advice is to keep the organisational phrases simple e.g. "I'm going to talk about the film... I saw this film last year with one of my friends..." Hope this helps
