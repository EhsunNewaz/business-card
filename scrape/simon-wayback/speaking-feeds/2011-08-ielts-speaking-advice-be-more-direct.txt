2011-09-09T11:34:57Z :: 2011-09-09T11:34:57Z :: Simon :: Hi Daniel, I think you have the wrong idea about 'content'. It's true that you don't need to show deep knowledge, and you can express any opinion you want. However, the examiner considers 'content' in a different way: you must be able to discuss topics and express RELEVANT ideas. In this sense, content cannot be separated from language ability because you demonstrate your language ability by expressing relevant ideas. / Remember, my argument here is not against grammar or pronunciation, it's against over-use of linking, filling, connecting phrases: / Have a look at the band 5 description for fluency and coherence: "may over-use certain connectives and discourse markers". Compare that to band 7 lexical resource: "uses vocabulary resource flexibly to discuss a variety of topics". The band 5 description is about linking and filling, whereas the band 7 description is about relevant content. / You'll just have to take my word for it on this. I'm afraid examiners think like I do.

2011-09-06T10:36:02Z :: 2011-09-06T10:36:02Z :: Simon :: Hi Daniel, The problem is that many students put all of their effort into remembering and saying these phrases. The result is that they focus more on the phrases and LESS on answering the question. / The advantage of being direct is that all of your effort is focused on answering the question (like it would be if you were speaking in your own language). Examiners are much more impressed if you do this. Examiners are not impressed by the phrases above because ANYONE can learn them. / Note: it's ok to use one or two of the above phrases once or twice in the test. The big problem is that many students use them at the start of every answer - this 'overuse' can be annoying for the examiner.
