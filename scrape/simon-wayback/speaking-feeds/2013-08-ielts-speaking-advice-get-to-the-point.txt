2013-08-27T17:46:13Z :: 2013-08-28T07:31:20Z :: Simon : FROM SIMON There's a useful discussion about all of this in the comments below this lesson: http://ielts-simon.com/ielts-help-and-english-pr/2013/08/ielts-speaking-avoid-these-phrases.html / Please read what Martin and sjm say in their comments. / Rita, / I disagree with the idea that native speakers rarely say "I think" (I say it quite a lot), but I agree that it's also common to say 'I suppose' 'I guess' or 'I would say'. We use these quick fillers so often that nobody really notices them. They also allow you to get to the substance of your answer so quickly that the filler itself becomes almost irrelevant. / My main message in these last two lessons has been this: don't waste time learning 'filler phrases'. Short, natural fillers are great (and the occasional long one is fine too), but your main focus should always be on the ANSWER, not the filler.
