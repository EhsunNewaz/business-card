# IELTS Advice: big words don't impress
- **Date:** 2019-05-19 | **আর্কাইভ লিংক:** [2019/05/ielts-advice-big-words-dont-impress.html](https://web.archive.org/web/20240617105415/https://www.ielts-simon.com/ielts-help-and-english-pr/2019/05/ielts-advice-big-words-dont-impress.html) | **Comments:** 18 (টপ ১০ #৫)

## Content
One of my IELTS examiner friends sent me a message the other day. Here's a short version of what he said:

"Who is teaching candidates words like 'plethora', 'myriad' and 'recapitulate'? These words stand out like a sore thumb."

As I've said many times before, individual big words won't impress the examiner. They're more likely to have the opposite effect.

6. **sjm** (2019-05-20): Everyone — the full Band 7 descriptor: "uses less common lexical items with some awareness of style and collocation". 1) Don't look at one part in isolation — 'style' and 'collocation' clarify it must be natural. 2) Descriptors work together ('precision'). 3) 'Less common' does NOT mean 'unusual' — it means compared to users in lower bands. Band 9 has no mention of 'extremely uncommon', only skilful and natural use. 'Recapitulate' would be unnatural for a Band 9 candidate in almost every situation.

'Practice' without expert feedback = practising the wrong things. As a rule: more than ~8 errors in Task 1 or ~12 in an essay → grammar/vocab slips below 7. Increase error-free sentences; learn your common errors and eliminate them.
